Sources and further study

This bibliography supports the historical framing and track choices in the chaptered Quarto source set anchored by edm_field_guide.qmd. Genre classifications are scene- and time-dependent; a link here supports a historical relationship or listening claim, not a claim that one source has final authority over a genre.

Metadata and platform audit notes

  • DJ Tameil’s “Dat Butt”: oral histories often place the self-release in 2001; catalog records may use 2002.
  • KinDzaDza’s “Dasty Ho”: use the original Waves from Outer Space CD version, not a similarly titled later issue.
  • Energy 52: use the Three ’N One Remix of “Café del Mar” from 1997, not an unspecified original or modern remake.
  • Age of Love: use the 1992 Watch Out for Stella Club Mix.
  • Tina Moore: use the Kelly G Bump-N-Go Vocal Mix.
  • Early white-label hardcore, Baltimore-club, bounce, and footwork records may have archival versions whose streaming dates reflect reissue rather than creation.
  • Spotify entity review is complete for all 158 rows: 146 requested recordings are accepted and playable in the authenticated United States catalog, while 12 requested versions are explicitly unavailable. The track-order-verified 146-track Spotify edition preserves manifest-relative order and omits those 12 rows rather than substituting neighboring edits, remixes, re-recordings, or DJ-mix excerpts. Spotify currently reports its profile visibility as public despite documented API privacy requests; the Spotify client’s Make private action remains pending.
  • The unavailable set is documented on the book’s Listen on Spotify page and in spotify_resolution.csv, including exact-but-unplayable entities for Shy FX & UK Apachi’s 1994 “Original Nuttah” and Oliver Heldens’s full original “Gecko.”
