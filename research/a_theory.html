A-theory

A-theory has become the name for a new approach to the fundamental description of string/M-theory I started developing in collaboration with Warren Siegel in mid/late 2014 which incorporates the duality symmetries encoded in M-theory directly on the strings.

The idea for it was originally inspired by the following things:

  1. In the 2-derivative approximation, the low-energy limit of string theory is supergravity. The simplest string theories have flat 10-dimenionsional target spaces, and there are five consistent possibilities called type I, type IIA, type IIB, heterotic $SO(32)$, and heterotic $E_8\times E_8$. The type IIA string looks suspiciously like a low-energy limit of some eleven-dimensional theory with the extra dimension rolled up. 11D supergravity is unique and any higher dimension-if it corresponds to anything fundamental-seems to be substantially more complicated than the lower-dimensional cases. In 1978, it was discovered by Cremmer, Julia, and Scherk that 11-dimensional supergravity has a hidden symmetry of a special kind which in the math jargon is referred to as exceptional symmetry of type $E$ [1][2]. (This is the same $E$ as the $E_8$ above, but it plays an entirely differnent role.) The hidden symmetry can be made explicit by rolling the theory up on a higher-dimensional donut (techically an $n$-torus). The higher the dimension of the donut, the more symmetry emerges.
  2. Michael Duff had conjectured in 1983 that actually this is backwards and we should think of the exceptional symmetry of type E as akin to a spacetime symmetry [3]: a "more correct" formulation of M-theory should have the most E symmetry when it also has the most spacetime symmetry (i.e. the smallest-dimension donut).
  3. In 1993 Warren had given a description of superstrings with manifest T-duality [4].
  4. In 1995 Edward Witten gave a series of strong arguments suggesting that there is a more "primitive" theory he called M-theory that has all the string theories as special limits [5]. This theory also describes 11-dimesional supergravity in a specific limit.
  5. In 1996 Vafa conjectured that there should be a theory akin to Witten's M-theory that lives in 12 dimensions and manifests the symmetries of the type IIB string geometrically [6]. This is by analogy of the symmetries of type IIA are described as 11D supergravity on a small circle. But just as 11D SG is a limit of some "real thing" Witten called M-theory, there should be a "real thing" for this case that Vafa called F-theory. Just as M-theory has extended objects more general than strings, according to Vafa's original conjecture, F-theory be based on objects more general than strings. (His specific idea was that the thing replacing the string should be twice the dimension.)

Vafa's original idea did not immediately seem to pan out, and the research community eventually abandoned it. What had proved fruitful, however, was the perspective Vafa's point of view gave on type IIB string profiles with non-constant dilaton, and these string solutions were what Vafa had originally meant when he referred to them as "Evidence for F-theory". Unfortunately, this finer point was not appreciated by the community at large, and the term "F-theory" became synonymous with type IIB on elliptically fibered Calabi-Yau 4-folds with D7-branes.

Somewhat belatedly, Warren and I discovered that a faithful representation of Vafa's original idea could be obtained by extending his previous work on superstrings with manifest T-duality to the E-type algebras. Perhaps not unrelatedly, a cottage industry had developed trying to reformulate classical supergravity theories using the E-type symmetries as a spacetime symmetry to organize classical supergravity compactifications.
